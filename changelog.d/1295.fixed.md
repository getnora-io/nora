**A cached npm packument keeps the validators of its own body when two requests revalidate it at once (#1295)** — NORA stores a cached packument, the record of where it came from and its upstream validators (`ETag`, `Last-Modified`) as separate writes, in the background after the response. When two requests for one package revalidated it at the same time, a request whose answer was `304 Not Modified` could write the validators of the copy it held after a parallel `200` had stored a newer document, leaving the older document's validators next to the newer body; later revalidations then asked the upstream about a document other than the one stored. The writes of a package's cached documents now take the lock its publish and retention take, so they wait for each other; a body stored without validators drops those another body left; a `304` writes nothing when the stored body or its record is no longer the copy it confirmed, and clears the validators of a missing body only while it is still missing; and the cache write of a tarball request's metadata finishes even when the request is dropped. The writes of different packages do not wait for each other, and a `304` still reads the body once and uploads nothing.
