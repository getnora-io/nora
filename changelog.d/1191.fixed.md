**A Docker upstream that rate-limits NORA is a `429` that says so, not a missing image (#1191)** — when an upstream answered `429 TOOMANYREQUESTS` (Docker Hub over its pull limit), a manifest or blob NORA did not have cached was answered `404 MANIFEST_UNKNOWN` / `BLOB_UNKNOWN`, so `docker pull` printed `manifest unknown` for an image that exists, no client retried, and the `Retry-After` was dropped. Such a miss is now `429` with `Retry-After` and an OCI `TOOMANYREQUESTS` body that names the upstream and the wait; `docker pull` prints `toomanyrequests: …`, and containerd, BuildKit, skopeo and podman retry it (skopeo and podman after the `Retry-After`). The wait is the upstream's `Retry-After` in seconds, at most 60 s (skopeo and podman wait no longer); without one in seconds it is 30 s. Until it has passed NORA does not ask that upstream again: containerd and BuildKit retry a `429` at once, five or six times, and each retry would have gone to the upstream that is already refusing. In the fallback chain the limited upstream is skipped, as one with an open circuit breaker is, and the next one can still serve the image; when none serves it, the `429` decides over another upstream's `404`, since the image may be on the limited one. A `429` does not count toward opening the circuit breaker. The wait is kept per NORA process, as the breaker is. A `429` from an upstream's token server is still answered `502`.
