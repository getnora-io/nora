**A failed upstream is answered 502, not 404, in Maven, Docker, Cargo, PyPI and Terraform (#1073)** — when an artifact was not cached and every upstream failed (5xx, refused, timed out), these formats answered 404, which clients take as "does not exist": Maven records it in the local repository and keeps failing after the upstream is back until the update interval passes or `-U` is used. They now answer 502, as npm, Go, RubyGems, Conan, Ansible, CPAN and pub already did; a 404 still means an upstream answered that the artifact is not there (with several upstreams, one such answer is enough). NuGet, Debian and RPM keep 404, because their clients retry a 5xx several times per package. An installation that is offline on purpose should remove its default upstreams, so that a miss stays a 404.
